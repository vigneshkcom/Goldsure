Live Diagnosis

Walk through each step with the customer. Click an answer to reveal the next question.

Follow steps in order - each answer reveals the next question.
Step 1
01Is the Alarm Currently Sounding or Flashing Red Rapidly?ACTIVE
Say to customer "Could you walk around and check each alarm? Is there any unit with a fast-flashing red light, or is any alarm actively beeping loudly right now?"
↳Active Alarm - Smoke or NuisanceALARM
⚠ Active Alarm Detected
Before treating as a nuisance, the customer must confirm there is no actual fire or smoke source first.
Say to customer "First - please quickly check there's no actual fire or smoke in your home. If everything's clear and it's a nuisance alarm from steam, dust, or cooking, press the Test/Hush button once to silence it for up to 10 minutes. Then use a soft brush or vacuum to gently clean the vent slots around the outside of that unit."
↳Post-Clean - Run Radio Link TestACTIVE
Say to customer "Let's confirm the whole system is still linked. Press and hold the button on any one alarm for about 1 second, then release. After a short delay, every other paired alarm should flash green and beep 6 times. Did all the others respond?"
↳Persistent Alarm - EscalateESCALATE
Escalate - Alarm Persists After Cleaning
If the alarm keeps re-sounding after the 10-minute hush expires even with no smoke source, the sensor may be contaminated or faulty. Do not advise the customer to disconnect power.

Log as: Persistent nuisance alarm - cleaning ineffective.
02What Is the Customer's Concern?ACTIVE
Note for agent: No fast-flashing red simply means no alarm is currently triggered - this is completely normal. The alarms are not faulty. Now find out what the customer is actually seeing or experiencing.
Say to customer "That's fine - none of the alarms are in active alarm mode right now, which is perfectly normal. To help you further, could you tell me: are any of the alarms showing any kind of light, or making an occasional beeping or chirping sound? Even if it only happens every minute or so?"
↳Alarm Triggered Earlier - Check for Memory IndicatorINVESTIGATE
Note for agent: When an alarm triggers and then returns to normal, it leaves a memory indicator - a slow red flash every 20 seconds. Check if this is visible.
Say to customer "Is any alarm showing a slow red flash - one blink roughly every 20 seconds? That would be the alarm memory indicator, letting you know it was triggered recently."
↳Alarm Memory - Not a FaultINFO
Alarm Memory Indicator - Unit Is Working Correctly
Red LED flashing once every 20 seconds means this unit was triggered recently but has since returned to normal standby. It is a reminder only - there is no current fault.

To clear: Press the Test/Hush button once. The slow red flash stops immediately. Memory also clears automatically after 12 hours.
Say to customer "That slow red flash every 20 seconds is just a memory reminder - it means the alarm was triggered recently but it's fully back to normal now. There is nothing wrong with the unit. To clear that light, just press the button on that alarm once. I'd also suggest cleaning the vent slots with a soft brush to reduce the chance of nuisance triggers in future."
↳Low Battery - Unit Replacement NeededESCALATE
Low Battery - Full Unit Replacement Required
Yellow LED flashing once every 54 seconds + 1 beep every 54 seconds = internal 10-year lithium battery is running low.

The battery is sealed and non-replaceable. The entire unit must be replaced.

Interim: Customer can press Test/Hush once to silence chirping for 12 hours. Yellow LED will continue flashing silently.
Say to customer "That yellow light flashing once with a single beep every 54 seconds means the internal battery is running low. The battery in this unit is sealed and can't be replaced on its own - we'll need to arrange a full replacement unit for you. In the meantime, press the button once to silence the chirping for up to 12 hours."
↳Sensor Malfunction - Clean First, Then MonitorCLEAN FIRST
Sensor Malfunction - Clean Unit Then Monitor
Yellow LED flashing twice every 54 seconds + 2 beeps every 54 seconds = sensor fault detected.

Often caused by dust, debris, or insects. Clean first before escalating.

Customer can press Test/Hush once to silence for 12 hours. If fault persists more than 1 hour after cleaning, escalate.
Say to customer "That double yellow flash and double beep every 54 seconds means the sensor has detected a fault - this is often just dust or debris inside the unit. Please clean all the vent slots with a soft brush or vacuum. You can also press the button once to silence the beeping for 12 hours. If the double yellow flash is still showing after about an hour, please call us back."
↳Persistent Malfunction - EscalateESCALATE
Escalate - Sensor Fault Not Cleared By Cleaning
Cleaning did not resolve the fault after more than 1 hour. The sensor component may have failed. Arrange unit replacement.
↳End of Life - 10-Year Service Life ReachedREPLACE
End of Life - Unit Must Be Replaced Immediately
Yellow LED flashing 3 times every 54 seconds + 3 beeps every 54 seconds = the unit has reached its 10-year end of service life.

This is not fixable. Unit must be replaced.

Interim: Press Test/Hush once to silence for up to 24 hours - but only within the first 30 days past EOL. After 30 days, hush stops working entirely.
Say to customer "That pattern - yellow flashing 3 times every 54 seconds with 3 beeps - means this alarm has reached its end of service life after 10 years. This one needs to be replaced. You can press the button once to silence it for up to 24 hours, but we do need to arrange a replacement as soon as possible."
03Check Power SupplyACTIVE
Say to customer "Is this unit hardwired to mains power, or is it a battery-only model?"
↳AC Model - Verify Green Power LightACTIVE
Say to customer "On the front face of the alarm, do you see a solid, constant green light? Or is the green light off?"
↳No Mains Power - Check BreakerCHECK POWER
No Mains Power Detected
The green light should be solid and constant when AC power is present. No green light means the unit has lost mains power.
Say to customer "The alarm isn't receiving mains power. Please check your circuit breaker or fuse box and see if the circuit for the smoke alarms has tripped. Reset it by flipping it fully off then back on, and let me know if the green light comes back on."
↳Dead Unit - EscalateESCALATE
Escalate - Power Present But Unit Unresponsive
Circuit breaker is on but the alarm still shows no green light. A licensed electrician may need to inspect the wiring first. Escalate accordingly.
↳Battery Model - Confirm PowerACTIVE
Say to customer "Please tap the test button once quickly. You should see a single green flash - that confirms the battery has power. Did you see a green flash?"
04Full Radio Link TestACTIVE
Say to customer "Great. Now let's confirm all the alarms are talking to each other. Press and hold the button on any one alarm for about 1 second, then release. After a short delay, every other paired alarm should flash green and beep 6 times. Did all the other alarms respond?"
↳Pairing Issue - Re-pair or EscalateACTION NEEDED
One or More Alarms Not Responding to Link Test
A unit not responding is either not paired, or out of wireless range. Max range is ~150m in open air - walls and steel reduce this significantly.

See the Networking / Pairing tab for re-pairing steps. If range is the issue, the host device should be moved to the centre of the building.

✓ Case De-escalated

Important Notes
Wireless Limits & Placement
Max wireless: 40 alarms - exceeding this may cause permanent damage.
Max wired: 20 alarms over 250 metres.
Open air range: ~150 metres between any two units. Steel and thick walls reduce range significantly.
Host placement: Centre of building acts as repeater. 2-storey: centre of main floor. 3-storey: centre of middle floor.
Pair one at a time: Do not run code learning on multiple units simultaneously.

Installation Locations

Reference for nuisance alarm relocation advice - AS3786:2023 manual

Where to Install
Recommended Locations
• Between sleeping areas and potential fire sources (living rooms, kitchens)
• Hallway within 3m of any bedroom door
• Every level of a multi-storey home
• No more than 7.5m from the farthest wall, any room door, or the next alarm
• Centre of ceiling is ideal; if wall-mounted, 300mm below ceiling
• On sloped/peaked ceilings: 900mm from the highest point
Where NOT to Install
Avoid These Locations
• Within 400mm of an air-conditioning vent or ceiling fan blades
• In kitchens, boiler rooms, laundry rooms, or garages
• In very dusty or dirty areas
• Within 300mm (12 inches) of light fittings or room corners
• Within 1500mm (5 feet) of fluorescent light fittings
• At the peak of an A-frame ceiling ("dead air" zone)
• In insect-infested areas
• Near ceiling decorations that block the smoke path
• On poorly insulated walls or ceilings
• Over stairwells or locations that make testing hazardous
